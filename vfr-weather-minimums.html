THE IDEA TO TAKE AWAY

Identify the airspace, altitude, and day or night conditions before choosing a row of the weather-minimums table.

See what “3-152” is measuring

For basic VFR in Class C, Class D, and Class E below 10,000 feet MSL, the familiar pattern is 3 statute miles of flight visibility, 1,000 feet above clouds, 500 feet below clouds, and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds. The clearances are measured from the cloud to your airplane. 14 CFR 91.155

Choose the right row

This summary is for airplanes under basic VFR. SM means statute miles; MSL means mean sea level. The Class G height bands use height above the surface. Exceptions in § 91.155(b) and Special VFR in § 91.157 have separate conditions.

A METAR does not answer the whole question

Flight visibility and reported ground visibility are different measurements. Section 91.155(c) also limits VFR beneath a ceiling below 1,000 feet in controlled airspace designated to the surface for an airport, except as provided in § 91.157. Paragraph (d) adds visibility conditions for takeoff, landing, and entering the traffic pattern in that airspace. 14 CFR 91.155

Student pilots have additional limits: § 61.89(a)(6) bars acting as pilot in command when flight or surface visibility is below 3 SM during daylight hours or 5 SM at night. Logbook limitations can be tighter still. 14 CFR 61.89

Legal minimums are one part of planning.

Use current weather information and discuss personal minimums and route-specific decisions with your CFI. This guide teaches the rules; it is not a substitute for instruction or a flight briefing.

Pick the row before reciting the numbers

Check your understandingYou are in Class E at exactly 10,000 feet MSL. Which row applies?

The at-or-above row: 5 SM flight visibility, 1,000 feet below clouds, 1,000 feet above, and 1 SM horizontally. The boundary is inclusive.

14 CFR 91.155
Check your understandingDoes a 1 SM Class G daytime minimum let a solo student pilot use 1 SM visibility?

No. The student-pilot PIC restriction is additional to the airspace minimum. Flight and surface visibility must each meet the applicable student limit.

14 CFR 61.89
