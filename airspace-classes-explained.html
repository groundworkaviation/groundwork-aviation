THE IDEA TO TAKE AWAY

Class B needs an ATC clearance. Class C and D need established two-way communication before entry. Equipment and pilot requirements are additional checks.

Clearance and communication are different tests

For VFR entry, Class B requires an ATC clearance. Class C and D require you to establish two-way radio communication with the facility providing services before entry, then maintain communication while within the airspace. 14 CFR 91.131 14 CFR 91.130 14 CFR 91.129

The FAA AIM explains the callsign test for Class C: if ATC responds with your aircraft identification and tells you to stand by, radio communication has been established. A response to an unidentified aircraft does not establish it. A direction to remain outside must still be followed. The same communication distinction is useful when studying Class D. FAA AIM 3-2-4 and 3-2-5

Understand each class before memorizing a shape

A

High-altitude controlled airspace

Generally 18,000 feet MSL through FL600, inclusive. Operations are generally conducted under IFR. Study the geographic scope and authorization provisions in the FAA sources.

B

Busy airport areas

Individually tailored surface areas and shelves. VFR entry needs a clearance; a familiar “wedding cake” is a mental picture, not a universal set of dimensions.

C

Radar-service airport areas

Usually a surface core and an outer shelf. Establish two-way communication before entry; use the chart for actual limits.

D

Towered airport areas

Individually tailored around the airport. Establish two-way communication before entry and check published operating hours.

E

Other controlled airspace

Can begin at the surface or above it. Basic VFR generally does not require ATC contact just because you are in Class E.

G

Uncontrolled airspace

Airspace not designated A, B, C, D, or E. “Uncontrolled” does not remove weather minimums or the other rules that apply to your flight.

Class descriptions: FAA PHAK, Chapter 15. These are study summaries, not chart boundaries.

Read the chart in three passes

  1. Find the boundary. Identify the class and your planned position relative to its lateral limits.
  2. Find the floor and ceiling. Read the published altitude limits and their reference. A shelf can sit above you.
  3. Check the requirements. Review communication, pilot qualifications, equipment, weather, and current chart/NOTAM information with your CFI.

Transponder and ADS-B requirements have their own geographic rules and exceptions. A Class D label alone does not answer whether you need either on a particular route. Use §§ 91.215 and 91.225 with the chart. 14 CFR 91.215 14 CFR 91.225

Explain which permission is missing

Check your understandingYou have radio contact with ATC. Does that alone authorize VFR entry into Class B?

No. You need the Class B clearance. Radio contact and a clearance are separate requirements.
