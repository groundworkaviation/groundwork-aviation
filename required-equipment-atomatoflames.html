THE IDEA TO TAKE AWAY

A memory aid starts the equipment discussion. The rule, aircraft documents, and applicable maintenance requirements determine what is required.

Day VFR: use the mnemonic as an index

Section 91.205 covers powered civil aircraft with standard U.S. airworthiness certificates. The familiar ATOMATOFLAMES mnemonic helps recall many day-VFR items, but the regulation contains conditions and additional requirements. 14 CFR 91.205

Items the mnemonic can hide: paragraph (b)(14) requires shoulder harnesses/restraint systems for front seats in small civil airplanes manufactured after July 18, 1978, and all seats after December 12, 1986. Paragraph (b)(12) adds flotation gear when operated for hire over water beyond power-off gliding distance from shore, plus a pyrotechnic signaling device unless operating under part 121. Rotorcraft have a separate shoulder-harness provision in (b)(17). 14 CFR 91.205

Night VFR: add the requirements in (c)

F · Fuses

One spare set, or three spare fuses of each required kind, accessible in flight.

L · Landing light

One electric landing light if the aircraft is operated for hire.

A · Anticollision

An approved red or white system on all U.S.-registered civil aircraft; read (c)(3) for installation and failure provisions.

P · Position lights

Approved position lights.

S · Source of energy

Adequate electrical energy for all installed electrical and radio equipment.

Plus the day list

Paragraph (c)(1) includes the instruments and equipment specified in (b).

FLAPS is a study mnemonic for § 91.205(c), not a separate rule. An item can also be required by your aircraft’s documents or another regulation. 14 CFR 91.205 14 CFR 91.213

A missing item needs a separate check

First establish whether an approved MEL applies or the aircraft and operation qualify for the no-MEL route in § 91.213(d)(1). Under (d)(2), check the VFR-day type-certification requirements, equipment list/KOEL, § 91.205 and other applicable rules, and Airworthiness Directives. 14 CFR 91.213

The removal or deactivation route has its own placarding and maintenance-record conditions in (d)(3). Paragraph (d)(4) also requires the specified hazard determination by an appropriately certificated and rated pilot or maintenance person. Work through an actual discrepancy with your CFI and appropriately qualified mechanic; this overview is not a substitute for their guidance. 14 CFR 91.213

Explain the qualifier

Check your understandingIs a landing light always required by § 91.205 for night VFR?

Paragraph (c)(4) makes that particular requirement conditional on the aircraft being operated for hire. That does not settle other aircraft-specific requirements or what to do with an installed, inoperative light.
