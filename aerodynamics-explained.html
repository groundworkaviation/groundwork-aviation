THE IDEA TO TAKE AWAY

A stall is about exceeding the critical angle of attack. The airspeed at which that happens changes with weight, load factor, and configuration.

Start with a balanced picture

In straight-and-level, unaccelerated flight, lift balances weight and thrust balances drag. That particular flight condition matters: the same simple equalities do not describe every climb, turn, or acceleration. FAA PHAK, Chapter 5

Angle of attack is measured against the airflow

Angle of attack is the angle between the wing’s chord line and the relative wind. The chord line joins the leading and trailing edges. The relative wind follows the direction opposite the airplane’s flight path through the air. Pitch attitude and angle of attack therefore are not interchangeable. FAA PHAK, Chapter 5

A wing stalls when it exceeds its critical angle of attack. A stall can occur at any airspeed or attitude. A published stall speed is tied to stated test conditions; it is not a universal speed that protects every maneuver. The POH/AFM identifies the conditions behind its numbers. FAA PHAK, Chapter 5

More load means a higher stall speed

Load factor is the ratio of the load supported by the wings to the airplane’s weight. In a coordinated level turn, the wing must produce more lift as bank increases to maintain altitude. FAA PHAK, Chapter 5

Stall speed increases with the square root of load factor, with weight and configuration held constant. The square root of 2 is about 1.41. A 60-degree bank by itself does not imply 2 G; this example requires the coordinated, level-turn condition. FAA PHAK, Chapter 5

Learn the cause, then the procedure.

Reducing angle of attack is central to ending a stall. Practice recognition and recovery only with your CFI and the applicable aircraft procedures. A diagram is not a substitute for flight instruction.

Explain the relationship

Check your understandingCan an airplane stall while its airspeed is above a published 1 G stall speed?

Yes. Increased load factor can bring the wing to its critical angle of attack at a higher airspeed. Weight and configuration also affect stall speed.

FAA PHAK, Chapter 5
Check your understandingIs pitch attitude the same as angle of attack?

No. Pitch attitude describes the airplane relative to the horizon; angle of attack describes the wing chord relative to the relative wind.
